CONTAGION

Transmission of diseases, behaviours and emotions. Falling Walls Winner, Science Engagement, 2021.

ROLE
Programme Manager: curation, programming, online experience design
WHEN
30 April – 31 December 2021
FORMAT
Fully online, made fully remotely
AWARD
Falling Walls Winner 2021, Science Engagement

There have been pandemics before. Knowing that gave us hope that we could face this one together.

CONTAGION was the largest exhibition we made entirely remotely, during the COVID-19 pandemic. Sixteen interactive exhibits connected to lectures, tutorials, workshops, masterclasses, events and films, giving visitors many ways into the idea of contagion.

CURATORIAL APPROACH

Everything spreads

We designed CONTAGION around transmission in all its forms. Alongside the virus, the exhibition looked at misinformation (The Glass Room), computer viruses (Malware Museum), laughter (When the World Was a Laugh) and the histories of past epidemics, from cholera in London to plague in Bombay.

New commissions like Anaïs Tondeur's When the World Was a Laugh, which crowd-sourced laughter from around the world, and Fluid Dialogues by Basse Stittgen and T. Jayashree captured transmission in very different ways. Archival works by Ranjit Kandalgaonkar and Christos Lynteris showed how society lived through the plague.

Digital spaces can foster real connection and learning, even in isolation.

HIGHLIGHT

Social rooms and the Arcade

Curated collections of books, games and podcasts, cross-referenced to exhibits and programmes, gave visitors new ways into a difficult topic. Games in the Arcade, like The Wisdom and/or Madness of Crowds, surprised our young audiences and kept them coming back.

HIGHLIGHT

A live experiment

With UCL's Daniel Richardson and Parklife, visitors took part in a live online experiment on how behaviour spreads.
